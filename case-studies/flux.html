GLIDE

A mobile pop-up retail and delivery vehicle designed for dense urban environments — where the street is the store.

Role Industrial Designer
Year 2026
Duration 14 Weeks
Tools Rhino 3D, Keyshot, Figma
Focus Mobility, Retail, Urban
01 — Overview

Urban retail is broken for both vendors and customers.

Pop-up retail in dense cities is expensive, logistically fragile, and almost always ugly. Vendors carry too much, customers can't find them, and the experience feels improvised.

GLIDE was designed from the ground up as a purpose-built mobile retail and micro-delivery platform — a vehicle that makes the street feel like a flagship store.

Vehicle exterior — deployed configuration
02 — Design Intent

Form that communicates trust before a word is spoken.

The physical language of GLIDE borrows from Scandinavian micro-mobility and Japanese kei trucks — compact but complete. Every surface angle is intentional: the awning deploys in 8 seconds, the counter locks at exactly ergonomic height, the lighting system shifts from neutral white to warm amber at dusk automatically.

The goal was for GLIDE to feel like it belongs on a high street, not a car boot sale.

03 — Digital Layer

The vehicle and the app are one product.

Vendors manage their GLIDE through a companion app — route planning, inventory tracking, real-time customer proximity alerts, and sales data all in one place. The app interface mirrors the physical controls: the same gesture that deploys the awning in the app triggers it on the vehicle.

Customers can find active GLIDEs on a map, see live menus, and pre-order for pick-up — removing the worst friction in street retail: not knowing what's available.

04 — Outcome

A vehicle that made vendors feel like retailers.

Prototype user tests with 12 street vendors across three cities showed that GLIDE reduced setup time by 65% and increased customer dwell time by 40% compared to a standard market stall.

More importantly, vendors described feeling "professional" and "confident" — an emotional outcome that no brief can specify, but every good design should deliver.
