01 / Overview

What if a delivery vehicle could become a storefront?

A local business is built around its ability to reach customers. But while a fixed storefront provides a place to operate, it can also limit where the business meets its audience.

GLIDE explores a different role for urban mobility: a vehicle that transports products between locations and transforms into a temporary retail space when it arrives.

Core Concept

One vehicle. Two connected ways to do business.

  • Electric urban delivery vehicle during transit
  • Instant pop-up boutique storefront upon arrival
  • Modular illuminated shelving and customer service counter
  • Seamless daily vendor workflow from warehouse to market
Retail mode transformation Side panels and gullwing canopy deployed for pedestrian interaction

03 / Anticipated impact

From transporting products to creating a place to sell them.

GLIDE proposes a vehicle that combines two activities usually accommodated by separate spaces: delivery and retail. Its intended value lies in making the vehicle useful both while moving and when it reaches its destination.

These are intended benefits of the concept, not measured or validated business outcomes.

04 / The challenge

A vehicle moves products. A storefront presents them. Could one do both?

Delivery and retail place different demands on a product. A delivery vehicle needs to accommodate and transport goods. A retail environment needs to make products visible and accessible while supporting interaction with customers.

Mode A Demands

Delivery Requirements

Secure cargo containment, weatherproofing, low aerodynamic drag, efficient load-in/load-out paths, and urban maneuverability.

Mode B Demands

Retail Requirements

Open visibility, ambient lighting, eye-level product presentation, vendor-customer interaction space, and rapid physical setup.

The Design Question

"How might a vehicle support both product delivery and temporary retail, allowing local vendors to take their business beyond a fixed storefront?"

05 / Research & insights

The vehicle's role changes when it stops moving.

The project explored the relationship between the vendor, the products being transported, and the places where customers encounter the business. Three considerations shaped the design direction.

01 / Motion

Mobility & access

The vehicle must be compact enough for narrow urban streets, yet allow rapid access to products when stopped.

02 / Shift

Transit to display

Delivery prioritises carrying and protecting goods. Retail requires products to become immediately visible and accessible.

03 / Routine

The vendor's workflow

The experience extends beyond driving: arrive, unpack, calibrate displays, converse with buyers, and pack away cleanly.

04 / Spatial

Public realm fit

A stationary commercial presence must integrate respectfully with sidewalks, plazas, and pedestrian thoroughfares.

Key Insight

The transformation is not just a change in vehicle form. It is a change in how the vehicle is used.

06 / Design opportunity

Designing for two modes of operation.

The opportunity was to develop one product around two connected uses. This meant considering the vehicle's movement, internal organisation, transformation, and user interactions together.

Opportunity 01 & 02

Urban Mobility + Cargo Organisation

Design the vehicle around efficient transit between urban nodes, pairing high-density modular storage trays with a low-floor electric skateboard chassis.

Opportunity 03 & 04

Transformable Retail + User Interaction

Create an intuitive physical transformation sequence that unfolds into a warm, inviting retail pavilion in under two minutes with zero specialized tools.

07 / Physical product design

From cargo space to retail space.

The central design challenge was to translate the dual-purpose idea into a coherent vehicle architecture. I explored the relationship between the vehicle's exterior form, internal packaging, and transformable configuration through concept development, packaging drawings, and CAD.

The Defining Design Decision

The vehicle becomes more than a way to reach the destination. Its configuration gives the vendor a way to use that destination.

Technical CAD Packaging Architecture Wheelbase 3100 mm · Skate Chassis · Actuated Gullwing Canopy Linkage

Canopy Hinge Kinematics & LED Strip

CNC machined aluminum linkage elevates the gullwing canopy smoothly while integrating flush ambient illumination for evening retail.

Hardware Architecture

Storage, Access, and Display

The packaging exploration resolved three physical priorities simultaneously:

  • Transit containment: Rattle-free magnetic latches hold shelves securely while vehicle is in motion.
  • Extensible counter: Lowers to ergonomic standing height (920 mm) for customer transactions.
  • Weather shelter: Extended canopy protects both vendor and shoppers from rain and harsh sun.

09 / Final solution

One vehicle. Two connected uses.

GLIDE brings automotive form, product packaging, and transformable functionality into a single mobility concept. Rather than creating two separate products, GLIDE explores how one vehicle can accommodate both activities.

Dual Mode Comparison Left: Delivery Mode (Carry) ↔ Right: Retail Mode (Present)

Configuration B

Retail Mode — Present

10 / Outcome & learnings

Designing mobility around human activity.

GLIDE explored how a transformable vehicle could connect local product delivery with temporary retail. Through vehicle concept development, packaging drawings, CAD, and a storyboard-based service journey, the project considered both the physical product and the activities it was intended to support.

01 / Multifunctional scope

Delivery and retail introduce different spatial and operational requirements. A dual-purpose product must balance both without compromising either.

02 / Complete lifecycle

A vehicle designed for a business is not used only while moving. Arrival, setup, customer interaction, and departure are also part of its intended use.

03 / Transformative design

The transition between delivery and retail shapes the relationship between storage, display, and user interaction as a core mechanical decision.

04 / Systemic connection

Vehicle form, packaging, and the vendor journey can be considered together to explore how a physical product supports a broader commercial activity.
